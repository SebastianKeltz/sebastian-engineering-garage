Project brief

Project brief

The system began as a practical way to connect embedded firmware, sensor bring-up, serial diagnostics, and power-electronics protection in one documented build.

I separated the evidence into three categories—verified in hardware, simulated, and planned—so each claim is easy to evaluate and the unfinished work stays explicit.

Engineering challenge

Bring several low-voltage sensors into one reliable embedded platform while designing a 12 V inductive-load driver that protects the control electronics from switching transients.

Evidence map

What's proven—and what's next.

Claims are separated by evidence type so completed work, test results, simulations, and next steps stay clear.

Verified in hardware

Firmware and sensing

  • ESP32-S3 firmware upload, flash, PSRAM, and serial diagnostics completed.
  • BME280 detected at I²C address 0x76 with live temperature, humidity, and pressure readings.
  • PIR sensor events verified through GPIO 4 and serial output.

Simulated

Protected fan driver

  • MOSFET switching stage modeled for a 12 V fan load in LTspice.
  • The model produced an approximately 551 V drain spike without flyback protection.
  • Adding a flyback diode reduced the simulated drain-voltage transient.

In development

System integration

  • Build and validate the physical fan-driver stage on hardware.
  • Integrate additional light and entry-state sensing after independent verification.
  • Package the system and add a stable user-facing dashboard.

Build process

From first constraint to tested system.

A repeatable loop: isolate the problem, make the smallest useful version, observe it, and revise.

01

Board bring-up

Verified the ESP32-S3 environment, upload path, serial output, flash, and PSRAM before adding peripherals.

02

Sensor isolation

Tested each sensor independently, confirmed addresses and GPIO behavior, then combined the working paths.

03

Diagnostic firmware

Added readable serial states so wiring, initialization, and motion events could be checked quickly.

04

Protection study

Compared an inductive fan-driver model with and without flyback protection to quantify the switching transient.

05

Next integration

The next milestone is physical driver validation followed by enclosure and dashboard work.

Lessons

What changed my approach.

  • Bring-up is faster when every subsystem has an observable diagnostic state.
  • Inductive-load protection is a design requirement, not an optional cleanup step.
  • Clear evidence labels make an in-progress project more credible than vague completion claims.
